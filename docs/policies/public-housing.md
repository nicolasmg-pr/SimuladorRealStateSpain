# Public & protected housing construction

All URLs fetched 2026-08-07. Source rows registered in the session scratch file
`sources/policy-public-housing.md` (to be merged into `docs/sources.md`).

## 1. Mechanism

**The lever.** VPO (vivienda de protección oficial / vivienda protegida): dwellings built
under a public qualification that caps sale price or rent (a per-m² "módulo", e.g. Madrid
zone A max sale price 2,450 EUR/m² useful in 2024) in exchange for subsidies, cheap land
or soft credit. Two sub-levers with very different market effects:

- **Sale VPO** — subsidised owner-occupancy. Historically dominant in Spain. Adds owner
  supply but the unit can leave the protected stock (see decalification below).
- **Rental VPO / public rental** — dwellings held by public or non-profit landlords at
  capped rents. This is the sub-lever that builds a permanent social-rental stock.

**Who holds it.** Three-layer split: the State funds via the Plan Estatal de Vivienda
(now also builds directly via Sepes), the CCAA hold the housing competence and execute
(qualification, allocation, their own plans, e.g. Basque Plan Director, Madrid Plan Vive),
municipalities supply land and licences. Housing Europe notes the policy is "largely
devolved" — definitions and protection periods vary by CCAA
(https://www.housingeurope.eu/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/spain_the_state_of_housing_in_the_eu_2025_digital.pdf).

**The decalification story.** Spain built VPO at scale for decades — peak 225,000 VPO
started in 1973 alone (Catastro/Tinaut, https://www.catastro.hacienda.gob.es/documentos/publicaciones/ct/ct3/LA%20POL%C3%8DTICA%20ESTATAL%20DE%20LA%20VIVIENDA%20EN%20LOS%20A%C3%91OS%20OCHENTA%20Jos%C3%A9%20Justo%20Tinaut%20Elorza.pdf);
1984–86 VPO was 60.3% of all housing starts (same source); 1.5 M protected homes built
1991–2025 (Civio, https://civio.es/lo-publico/2026/05/21/evolucion-vpo-desde-1991-proteccion-en-vigor-por-comunidades-autonomas/).
But protection was time-limited (10–30 years; Madrid shortened some to 15) and most units
were for sale, so they leaked to the free market: >765,000 units deprotected since 2000
alone (aquimicasa/press, corroborated by Civio); of the 1.5 M built since 1991 roughly
half will have lost protection by end-2030, and counting early voluntary decalifications
the surviving protected stock is nearer 600,000 (~40% of those built) (Civio). Infobae
(citing sector estimates): without decalification the public/protected park would be
~3.8% of stock instead of ~2.5%
(https://www.infobae.com/espana/2025/10/07/el-parque-publico-de-vivienda-seria-del-38-si-no-se-hubieran-descalificado-miles-de-vpo-en-espana-hoy-apenas-llega-al-25/).
The Basque Country made qualification **permanent** in 2003 and keeps Spain's largest
protected stock; the Plan Estatal 2026–2030 now imposes permanent protection on all
state-financed units via property-registry notation (Provivienda,
https://provivienda.org/observatorio/plan-estatal-de-vivienda-2026-2030/).

**Current stock (verified, definitions matter).**
- Public social rental: ~318,000 dwellings = **1.7%** of stock (Housing Europe 2025,
  AVS estimate on INE Census 2021 base).
- Banco de España: ~300,000 units, **1.5%** of main dwellings, vs ~7% OECD/advanced-economy
  average (BdE via press; OECD Affordable Housing Database gives OECD average ~7%, EU ~8%
  — OECD PH4.2 PDF fetched but unparseable, averages taken from search excerpts, exact
  OECD point for Spain unverified).
- Total protected (sale + rental, all regimes): ~**3.4%** of stock (government estimate
  reported by Housing Europe 2025).
So the model should treat "Spain <3% social rental vs EU ~8–9%" as confirmed in order of
magnitude, with 1.5–2.5% the defensible range for social rental depending on definition.

**Current output.** Definitive VPO qualifications: 14,371 units in 2024 (best since 2014;
73.9% privately promoted), 11,104 in 2025 (−22.7%; 36.4% publicly promoted)
(MIVAU data via Forbes/Telemadrid/elEconomista). BBVA Research: ~13,000 subsidised
starts/yr since 2018, vs ~60,000/yr 1995–2002 and ~80,000/yr 2003–2010
(https://www.bbvaresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Low-supply-of-housing_Eng.pdf).
Public *rental* completions were historically tiny (486 units in all of Spain in 2014;
Springer JHBE 2016, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10901-016-9513-6).

**Current programmes (status as of fetch date).**
- **Sareb→Sepes transfer**: approved by Consejo de Ministros July 2025 — up to 40,000
  homes + 2,400 land plots (buildable capacity ~55,000 more homes), market value
  €5.9 bn, to the new state housing company built on Sepes; first tranche ~13,000 homes
  (of which ~13,000 of the 30,000 follow-on already occupied under social rents). Caveat:
  stock is concentrated in Catalonia (13,000), C. Valenciana (6,700), Andalusia (4,000);
  only ~10% sits in the 10 largest cities — poor spatial match with tensioned metros
  (idealista, https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/07/01/851594-sareb-traspasa-40-000-viviendas-y-2-400-suelos-a-la-empresa-publica-sepes; MIVAU).
- **Plan Estatal de Vivienda 2026–2030**: lines presented Sept 2025, approved RD 326/2026
  (April 2026). €7 bn total (60% State / 40% CCAA), ≥40% to new permanently-protected
  supply; aid up to 1,000 EUR/m² useful for promotion on public land (vs 700 in the
  2022–25 plan), up to 8,000 EUR/dwelling for land development, acquisition co-funding up
  to 70–85% (La Moncloa, MIVAU, Provivienda). The RD sets **no explicit unit target**;
  Housing Europe reports ministry programmes "aim to add ~300,000 social or affordable
  rental units by 2030" with targets "fragmented across regions".
- **Reserves on new development**: Barcelona (2018) requires 30% of new/renovated
  floorspace as VPO in consolidated urban land (upheld by Tribunal Supremo, Feb 2026).
  Basque land law 2/2006 requires 75% of new developable residential capacity (40% of
  added capacity on urban land) as protected housing in municipalities >3,000 inhabitants
  (UPC working paper; Basque Government). Catalonia's rent-pressure municipalities have
  similar 30% rules spreading (Terrassa, Ripollet — Foment del Treball).
- **Basque plan**: Plan Director 2025–2027 targets 7,000 affordable rentals/legislature
  (~1,700/yr in execution 2026–28), Pacto Social target 50,000 protected rentals by 2035
  (protected park 74,000 → 116,000) (euskadi.eus).

## 2. Evidence for

- **Scale works where sustained — Vienna.** ~43% of Vienna's dwellings are municipal or
  limited-profit cost-rent housing (GPLA overview, CIRIEC WP 2022/04). Limited-profit
  (LPHA) rents average €5.8/m², roughly 50% below for-profit levels; the Climate &
  Community Institute review reports an estimate that each 10 pp of cost-based stock
  lowers market rents ~€0.40/m², implying ~15% lower overall rents at Vienna's ~40%
  share (CCI, https://climateandcommunity.org/research/vienna-green-social-housing/ —
  headline figures from search excerpt; underlying WIFO-type study not fetched, treat as
  one estimate, not a consensus). **Structural caveat for the model**: Vienna's effect
  comes from a *stock* built over a century plus permanent cost-rent rules; it is not a
  flow effect a 5-year plan can reproduce. Critics also note quality gaps in the old
  Gemeindebau stock and insider/outsider allocation (Warsaw Enterprise Institute,
  https://wei.org.pl/en/2025/news/mgundinger/a-critical-examination-of-the-viennese-housing-model/).
- **France (HLM + SRU quota).** Social housing ~16% of stock (2022), 4.6 M households.
  The SRU law (2000; 20→25% municipal quotas) measurably evened out social housing across
  municipalities — it raised production where there was little (Housing Studies 2023
  evaluation, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02673037.2021.1941790;
  Freemark). Caveat: 1,222 municipalities still non-compliant; fee-paying municipalities
  built *less* than exempt ones relative to pre-law trend (same evaluation).
- **Spanish history shows the lever can move total supply.** When VPO was the dominant
  regime (60% of starts 1984–86; ~396 VPO per 100k inhabitants/yr in 1975–82 vs 18 in
  2023 — CoHispania, https://www.cohispania.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Informe-CoHispania.-REFLEXIONES-SOBRE-LA-VPO-EN-ESPANA.pdf),
  it stabilised housing output during the early-1980s slump: the 1981–83 Plan Trienal
  financed 512,200 units (90% of target) and "stabilised the VPO market and slowed the
  overall fall in housing starts" (Catastro/Tinaut). VPO prices ran 42.5–62.7% of free
  prices 2005–2023 (CoHispania), i.e. the qualified stock itself delivers a large
  affordability discount to occupants even when spillovers are uncertain.
- **No observed substitution in Spanish panel data.** Blanco-Arana (2023, e-pública /
  Dialnet, https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/9041025.pdf), CCAA panel
  2008–2022: VPO and free construction are *positively* correlated (significant at
  national and CCAA level) — in Spanish data the two move together over the cycle rather
  than substituting. (This is correlation across a demand-driven cycle, not causal
  identification, but it argues against assuming large crowd-out at current tiny scale.)
- **Scale needed for a price effect is known.** Banco de España: converging to the
  advanced-economy public-stock average requires ~1.5 M additional social rentals; over
  10 years that means raising total annual housing production >150% (BdE via elmon/OCU
  press summaries; BdE Annual Report 2023 ch.4 gives the supply context: ~90,000
  completions/yr since 2021 vs 650,000 at the 2006–08 peak,
  https://www.bde.es/f/webbe/SES/Secciones/Publicaciones/PublicacionesAnuales/InformesAnuales/23/Files/InfAnual_2023_Cap4_En.pdf).
  BdE (2025 report, press): housing deficit ~600,000 units by 2025, ~750,000 accumulated
  by 2026; Housing Europe cites BdE needing 1.2 M homes by 2030 (800k affordable + 400k
  social).

## 3. Evidence against / side effects

- **Crowding out (US evidence).** Sinai & Waldfogel (NBER WP 8709, 2002, verified):
  on average **three government-subsidised units displace two private units** (net +1/3
  per unit); crowd-out is lower in bigger markets and where excess demand for public
  housing is high; tenant-based vouchers target better than project-based construction
  (http://piketty.pse.ens.fr/fichiers/SinaiWaldfogel2002.pdf). Murray (1999, VAR on 27
  years): deep-subsidy *public* housing added to the total stock (≈no crowd-out), but
  moderate-income subsidised units were fully crowded out (via JCHS/Harvard review,
  https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/sites/jchs.harvard.edu/files/rr07-3_ellen.pdf). Eriksen &
  Rosenthal (2010) find large LIHTC crowd-out (via Soltas JMP). Range across literature:
  **0 to ~1.0 crowd-out per unit**, lower for deep-subsidy rental aimed at households the
  market does not serve, higher for near-market subsidised ownership — directly relevant
  since Spanish sale-VPO is the near-market kind.
- **Capacity competition (Spain-specific, current).** BBVA Research and BdE both document
  a binding construction-capacity constraint (labour: construction ~6.5% of employment vs
  9–12% historically; starts per capita among Europe's lowest since 2008). Public
  programmes hiring the same builders and buying the same land compete with private
  output when capacity binds — argument made by developer bodies; no Spanish causal
  estimate found (unverified as an effect size).
- **Decalification leakage.** Historically the dominant failure mode: >765,000 units
  deprotected since 2000; ~half of the 1991–2025 cohort gone by 2030 (Civio). Sale-VPO
  with time-limited protection is partly a *transfer* to first buyers (capitalised
  windfall at decalification), not a permanent stock addition. New permanent-protection
  rules close this for future units but do nothing for the existing cohort.
- **Misallocation / queues.** 180,000–250,000 households on social-housing waiting
  lists, waits up to 5 years in large cities (Housing Europe 2025). Allocation is by
  registry + lottery/points (e.g. Etxebide, Madrid sorteos); Civio (Sept 2025,
  https://civio.es/lo-publico/2025/09/23/vivienda-protegida-para-rentas-superiores-a-la-media-y-precios-parecidos-a-los-de-mercado/)
  documents rental-VPO in some regions reaching above-median-income tenants at
  near-market rents — targeting leakage at the top.
- **Slow delivery.** Cámara de Cuentas de Andalucía audit (via El País,
  https://elpais.com/diario/2008/06/01/andalucia/1212272523_850215.html): public VPO
  promotions averaged **6 years from land to completion** (up to 8, Emvisesa Sevilla;
  min 3, Jerez); mean 2,081 days land-to-keys; 688 days from allocation to key handover.
  UPM study of nine public promotions: 7 of 9 late, mean execution overrun +17% time,
  +4.5% cost (https://polired.upm.es/index.php/anales_de_edificacion/article/view/4456).
  Recent PPP is faster on ready land: Madrid Plan Vive launched 2020, >5,200 rentals
  delivered by ~2025–26, i.e. ~3–5 years (Comunidad de Madrid / idealista). State-level
  lag compounds: new state company announced Jan 2025, transfer approved Jul 2025; Plan
  2026–2030 presented Sept 2025, approved Apr 2026 — ~1.5 years before a single new
  euro flows.
- **Barcelona 30% reserve — both readings.** Output is undisputed and tiny: 11 licences
  with the reserve Dec 2018–Jul 2022 yielding ~52 protected units (Foment del Treball /
  APCE); ~144–150 units projected and only ~22 finished after seven years
  (IDRA count / Metropoli Abierta 2026). *Developer/employer reading* (Foment, APCE):
  the rule kills project viability — licence applications fell ~55% in the first year
  and investment moved to other municipalities; the failure is the rule. *Housing-
  movement reading* (IDRA researchers Ill-Raga, Palomera, Ciutad via El Salto): the rule
  was gamed and under-enforced — 7,509 units were licensed in the pre-rule rush/moratorium
  window, fraudulent permit-splitting hid ≥66 units in 17 inspected buildings, and the
  Collboni government's reform would shrink affected buildings from 3,909 to 95. The
  Supreme Court upheld the rule itself (Feb 2026, eldiario.es). Both agree on ~0 units;
  they disagree on whether the binding constraint is viability or enforcement. The model
  should be able to produce both worlds.
- **Fiscal cost per unit.** No single verified national figure. Anchors: Plan 2026–2030
  subsidy up to 1,000 EUR/m² useful (~70,000–90,000 EUR/unit of subsidy on a 70–90 m²
  unit) plus land; Sareb portfolio implicit value €5.9 bn for 40,000 homes + 2,400 plots
  (~€105k/home blended); VPO tasación ~1,131 EUR/m² (2020, CoHispania). All-in production
  cost per new public rental unit of **~120,000–200,000 EUR** is our derived range —
  label: guess (derived from module prices and subsidy caps, no audited per-unit source).

## 3b. Scale, speed and the Spanish baseline (Funcas 104 ch.2, ch.6, ch.8)

Added 2026-08-14.

- **Baseline stock.** Social rental is 1% of the Spanish stock (OECD 2023, ch.6) or 2.5%
  (Provivienda, ch.8), against 7% across developed countries and 9.3% in the EU. The model's
  `public_rental_share` = 0.017 sits between the two Spanish readings; the documented range
  widens to 0.010–0.025.
- **Historical counterfactual.** 29% of the park built since the 1980s was created under
  public protection programmes, which combined below-market prices with interest subsidies and
  tax relief — and those dwellings became fungible with free-market stock after a holding
  period, which is why the parque público never accumulated [ch.4].
- **Speed is not a law of nature.** Canada's Rapid Housing Initiative delivered **>10,000
  dwellings in six months** by collapsing administrative steps into a single window [ch.2].
  The model's `public_delivery_lag` range of 12–32 ticks (3–8 years) reflects Spanish practice,
  not a physical bound; a streamlined-delivery scenario belongs at the bottom of that range.
- **Land is the input that decides the rent.** Ley del Suelo reserves 40% of weighted
  buildability on rural-land actions and 20% on urban renewal for protected housing, plus a
  5–15% municipal land cession (historically 10%) — but regional law may let developers
  substitute a cash payment, so the reserve only becomes housing where there is political will
  [ch.4 note 3]. Current pipeline for comparison: SEPES activating public land for >16,840
  affordable dwellings, and a plan for 184,000 affordable rental homes via MIVAU, Sareb and
  the Fondo Social [ch.8].
- **Warning kept from the same source**: public supply helps distributionally but only relieves
  aggregate scarcity if it adds to total supply — which is what `crowding_out_share` is for
  [ch.2].

## 4. Effect-size range

The model must be able to reproduce all of the following, not a chosen point:

| Quantity | Range | Anchors |
|---|---|---|
| Net stock added per public unit built (1 − crowd-out) | **0.2 – 1.0** | Sinai-Waldfogel 1/3; Murray ≈1.0 for deep-subsidy rental; Blanco-Arana Spain co-movement ≈ no crowd-out; capacity-constrained Spain plausibly mid-range |
| Market-rent effect of cost-rent stock share | **0 to −15% at 40% share**; roughly −0.3 to −0.5%/pp of stock share, near-zero below ~5% share | Vienna estimate (CCI, one study, unverified primary); Spain’s 1.5–2.5% share too small for measurable city-level effect today |
| Occupant affordability discount (VPO vs free price/rent) | **−37% to −57%** (VPO price 42.5–62.7% of free) | CoHispania 2005–2023 series; Vienna LPHA rents −50% |
| Annual output, status quo | **11,000 – 14,500 qualifications/yr** (2024–25), of which public promotion 3,700–4,000 | MIVAU via press; BBVA ~13k starts/yr since 2018 |
| Annual output, announced ambition | **~40,000 – 60,000/yr equivalent** (300k by 2030; BdE convergence path 150k/yr for 10 yrs is the outer bound) | Housing Europe/MIVAU; BdE |
| Historical ceiling (feasibility proof) | **60,000 – 225,000/yr** | 1995–2010 starts; 1973 peak |
| Delivery lag, land-to-keys | **12 – 32 quarters** (3–8 yrs), median ~23 q; +17% mean schedule overrun | Andalusian audit; UPM; Plan Vive PPP at the fast end |
| Decalification leakage (legacy stock) | **~2–3%/yr of protected stock** (≈30k/yr avg since 2000); 0 for new permanent-protection units | Civio; 765k since 2000 |
| Inclusionary reserve output (Barcelona-type 30%) | **≈0 – 334 units/yr** promised vs 52–150 in 7 yrs realised; licence flow −10% to −55% (disputed attribution) | Foment/APCE vs IDRA |

Disagreements kept as ranges: crowd-out (US project-based vs Murray vs Spanish
co-movement); Barcelona licence collapse (viability vs evasion); Vienna spillover
(single-study estimate); social-rental stock share (1.5 vs 1.7 vs 2.5% by definition).

## 5. Model mapping

`scenario.Intervention` parameters (all rates per quarter; zone = tensioned metro /
secondary city / rural):

- `public_units_per_quarter: dict[Zone, int]` — new public/protected units *started*.
  Baseline calibration (status quo ≈ 12k/yr national): tensioned 1,800, secondary 900,
  rural 300 per quarter. Announced-plans scenario (≈45k/yr): tensioned 7,000, secondary
  3,000, rural 1,250. Historical-ceiling stress test: 4–5× that. Spatial caveat: the
  Sareb stock scenario loads *rural/secondary*, not tensioned zones (only ~10% in big
  cities) — allow zone weights to be set independently of the national total.
- `rental_share: float` — fraction of new public units that are permanent rental VPO
  (rest = sale VPO subject to leakage). Historical Spain ≈ 0.1–0.3; current policy
  design ≈ 0.8–1.0. Sale units enter owner stock and later leak via decalification.
- `crowding_out_share: float` — private starts displaced per public start.
  **Range 0.0–0.8**; suggested sweep {0.0, 0.33, 0.67}; make it endogenous-capable:
  higher when the zone's construction-capacity utilisation is high (Spain 2025-like),
  lower for deep-subsidy rental than for near-market sale VPO.
- `delivery_lag_quarters: int` — start (budget committed) to keys. **Range 12–32**,
  default 20 (≈5 yrs); PPP-on-public-land variant 12; add optional
  `announcement_lag_quarters` (4–8) for programme ramp-up before first starts, matching
  the 2025–26 state track record.
- `decalification_rate_per_quarter: float` — legacy sale-VPO reverting to free market.
  Range 0.005–0.0075/quarter (≈2–3%/yr) applied to the legacy protected stock;
  0 for new units if `permanent_protection=True` (Plan 2026–2030, Basque rule).
- `vpo_rent_discount` / `vpo_price_discount: float` — occupant-facing cap vs market:
  price 0.42–0.63 of free-market; rent ≈ 0.5 (range 0.4–0.7). Feeds `metrics.py`
  affordability indicators; the *market* rent spillover must emerge from the clearing
  mechanism (extra rental supply), not be imposed — validate that at Vienna-like shares
  the model produces something inside 0 to −15%.
- `inclusionary_reserve_share: float` (0, 0.3, 0.75) with `reserve_compliance: float`
  (0.0–1.0) — Barcelona/Basque reserves. `reserve_compliance` is the honest encoding of
  the Foment-vs-IDRA dispute: low compliance ≈ evasion world; viability world is
  reproduced by letting developers' `decide()` drop projects whose margin under the
  reserve goes negative.
- Fiscal accounting: `public_cost_per_unit_eur`: 120,000–200,000 (guess-labelled),
  subsidy component 70,000–90,000 (sourced to Plan aid caps) — for the government
  actor's budget constraint, units: EUR/dwelling.
