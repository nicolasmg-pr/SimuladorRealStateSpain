# Tourist-rental restriction

Short-term / tourist rental (VUT — vivienda de uso turístico) restrictions in Spain:
licensing caps, moratoria, and phase-outs. Researched 2026-08-07. All effect estimates are
recorded as ranges; disagreements are kept, not resolved.

## 1. Mechanism

VUT regulation in Spain is layered: CCAA define the legal figure and registries, municipios
control urban-planning permission, and since 2025 the State adds a single registry.

- **Barcelona (PEUAT + phase-out).** The PEUAT zoning plan froze new VUT licenses in the
  central zones from 2017. Catalan Decret llei 3/2023 converted existing HUT licenses into
  5-year urban-planning licenses; Barcelona announced (2024-06-21, Mayor Collboni, Pla Viure)
  it will **not renew** them, so all ~10,100 licenses (10,101 in force May 2025 per
  Ajuntament) expire by **November 2028**, and PEUAT will keep no VUT category at all.
  Status verified 2026: the Constitutional Court upheld the Catalan decree in 2025; the
  phase-out is on track and neighbouring metro municipalities (L'Hospitalet, Cornellà,
  Esplugues, Sant Adrià…) announced they will follow. The decree allows a one-time
  extension of up to 5 more years if the holder proves the transition period does not
  compensate the loss of the title — a contested escape valve, not automatic.
- **Madrid (Plan RESIDE).** Approved 2025-05, in force 2025-08: bans "dispersed" VUT in
  residential buildings in the historic centre (whole-building conversions only, tied to
  rehabilitation, 15-year reversible licenses); outside the centre VUT need independent
  access on ground/first floor. Sanctions hardened, inspector headcount +15%. Madrid's prior
  2019 Plan Especial de Hospedaje (independent-entrance rule) was widely evaded — see §3.
- **Canarias.** Vivienda vacacional law (approved Nov 2025): 5-year moratorium on new
  licenses until municipal plans adapt; municipalities may cap VUT at 10% of residential
  buildability (20% on La Palma / La Gomera / El Hierro); new-build homes must stay
  residential ≥10 years before conversion; compliant existing VUT may operate to 2035.
- **Baleares.** Decree-law of 2025-04-08 ("contención turística"): prohibition of new
  vacation-rental places in multi-family buildings; retired places parked in temporary
  pools pending island carrying-capacity reviews. Builds on the 2022 general moratorium on
  new tourist places.
- **National single registry.** Real Decreto 1312/2024 (23 Dec 2024, BOE-A-2024-26931) —
  verified: creates the Registro Único de Arrendamientos and the Ventanilla Única Digital
  for short-term rental data exchange (implements EU Reg. 2024/1028). In force 2025-01-02;
  registration number mandatory and platform data-sharing effective **2025-07-01**.
  Platforms must not advertise units without a valid number.
- **Horizontal-property law.** Ley Orgánica 1/2025 (disp. final 4ª) amends art. 17.12 LPH,
  in force **2025-04-03**: new VUT activity requires **prior express approval by 3/5 of
  owners and 3/5 of quotas** — the default flips from "allowed unless banned" to "banned
  unless approved". Not retroactive for VUT legally operating before that date.

## 2. Scale

- **Spain (INE experimental statistic, web-scraped from the 3 main platforms, half-yearly
  May/Nov):** ~368,295 VUT (Nov 2024) → peak ~382,000 (May 2025) → **329,764 (Nov 2025)**,
  a −12.4% y/y fall, −52,000 units May→Nov 2025 — the largest drop in the series. The
  Housing Ministry attributes the fall to the registry + LPH reform (self-crediting claim;
  substitution into "temporada" listings inflates it — see §3).
- **Barcelona:** 10,101 HUT licenses in force (May 2025, Ajuntament); Idescat counts
  11,717 "habitatges d'ús turístic" establishments in the Barcelona tourism brand area.
- **Madrid city:** 12,637 VUT advertised on platforms (Nov 2025, INE) vs only **1,283
  active VUT registry codes** in the state registry (MIVAU) — most advertised supply lacks
  a municipal license (see §3, enforcement gap).
- **Share of stock in hot zones — disputed.** INE dwelling-level data (via El País, 2025):
  VUT ≈ **2.8% of dwellings in Ciutat Vella** and 2.9% in Eixample (district-wide). But a
  cadastre + Inside Airbnb census-section analysis finds sections of central Barcelona
  where **~20% of apartments** are whole-unit Airbnbs (~30% including room rentals).
  Both are honest measurements at different granularity; the model should carry the range
  (district mean, few %) vs hotspot concentration (up to 20–30% of sections).

## 3. Evidence for / against

### Evidence for restrictions

- **Barcelona (primary study):** Garcia-López, Jofre-Monseny, Martínez-Mazza & Segú (2020,
  *Journal of Urban Economics* 119, 103278): Airbnb activity raised rents **+1.9%** and
  transaction (posted) prices **+5.3% (+3.7%)** in the average neighbourhood; in top-decile
  Airbnb neighbourhoods rents **+7%**, transaction prices **+17%** (posted +14%). A UPF
  replication-style thesis finds ~+2.1% rent per +100 listings in a neighbourhood, no
  significant transaction-price effect (magnitudes disagree on prices — keep both).
- **Madrid:** FEDEA working paper (2019, "The Airbnb Effect on the Rental Market: the Case
  of Madrid") attributes ≈ **+4% rents** to Airbnb (single study; magnitude unverified
  beyond abstract-level reporting — treat as one point in the range).
- **Portugal (Franco & Santos, 2021, *Reg. Sci. & Urban Econ.*):** +1pp Airbnb share →
  **+3.7% house prices** (municipality level; +2.9–3.2% at parish level in Lisbon/Porto);
  high-tourist parishes +24–32% vs low-tourist ones; **no significant rent effect** found
  (authors attribute this to a concurrent rent-liberalisation phase-out).
- **Lisbon ban (Gonçalves, Peralta & Pereira dos Santos, IZA DP 15706):** the 2018 ban on
  new AL registries in saturated zones (>25% STR ratio) caused **−8/−9% house prices** in
  treated zones (−20% for 2-bedroom units) — clean quasi-experimental evidence that the STR
  option is capitalised into prices, and that removing it lowers them.
- **Berlin (Duso, Michelsen, Schäfer & Tran, DIW / Reg. Sci. Urban Econ. 2024):** the 2016
  Zweckentfremdungsverbot strongly cut entire-home listings and **returned commercial
  units to the long-term market, lowering rents — "but not by much"**; only the reform that
  hit professional hosts moved long-term supply.

### Evidence against / side effects

- **Substitution to temporada / rooms:** in Barcelona, seasonal ("temporada") listings —
  outside both VUT licensing and the rent cap — reached ~64% of rental supply by end-2025
  (34% in March 2024) while permanent-rental supply halved (Idealista data; note this
  confounds the rent-cap and VUT policies). 2025 Barcelona inspections (>8,100): **71% of
  detected illegal VUT were disguised as temporada rentals**.
- **Enforcement gaps / illegal supply:** Madrid advertises ~12,600 VUT with ~1,300 valid
  codes (≈90% non-compliant with the state registry, though ~17,600 hold *some* code
  counting temporada). Amsterdam had ~6,000 illegal listings in 2017 despite Europe's
  strictest regime. German day-cap study (Bekkerman et al. 2024, *J. Acad. Marketing Sci.*):
  **>1/3 of hosts did not comply**.
- **Reverse experiments — did long-term supply return?**
  - **NYC Local Law 18** (enforced 2023-09): STR listings fell >80–90% (22–38k → ~3k legal).
    Two years on (WSJ 2025-09; Business Insider; Propmodo): rents **did not fall** — asking
    rents at record highs, Manhattan vacancy 1.4%; many delisted units shifted to 30+-day
    stays or stayed dark rather than returning to year-round leases. StreetEasy median
    asking rent +0.5% (Aug 23→Aug 24); other sources +2.1% (Oct 23→Oct 24). Tenant groups
    argue the counterfactual (rents would have risen more) is unobserved; Airbnb calls the
    law a failure. **Record both: the ban removed STRs but produced no measurable rent
    decline where STR share of stock was <2%.**
  - **Berlin 2016** is the positive counterpart: long-term supply measurably increased.
  - **Barcelona 2028** is pending — no outcome data yet; the 2025–26 fall in VUT coincided
    with a temporada boom, so net long-term supply gain is unproven.
  - German day caps: "few properties redirected to long-term rental… no significant drop
    in long-term rents"; hosts' welfare −9% to −46%, residents' gain "minimal".
- **License-value capitalisation:** the Lisbon study shows the license option is worth
  ~8–9% of price (20% for 2-bed). Barcelona brokers specialising in HUT sales claim premia
  of **25–35%** for licensed flats (industry claim, unverified), now decaying towards zero
  as November 2028 approaches — an expropriation-like loss concentrated on ~10k owners,
  fuelling litigation and extension requests under Decret llei 3/2023.
- **Tourism-economy effects (industry viewpoints, opposed to each other):** Exceltur
  (hotel-dominated lobby, favourable to VUT curbs) reports tourism GDP still grew 2.5% in
  2025 to €218bn while urban VUT fell −4.1% — i.e. restricting VUT did not hurt tourism.
  Airbnb-side: a PwC report commissioned by Airbnb claims STR caused only **0.3%** of
  2018–23 housing-cost increases (unverified primary; commissioned research); in NYC,
  tourist costs rose ~$91/trip and hotel RevPAR +7% after LL18 — restriction transfers
  surplus to hotels.
- **Limited effect where VUT share is small:** across all studies the rent/price effect is
  proportional to local STR density; in rural/secondary zones with <1% VUT share the
  expected effect is ≈0, while coastal/island and historic-centre hotspots carry it all.

## 4. Effect-size range

| Quantity | Range | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Rent effect per +1pp VUT share (symmetric assumption for removal) | **−0% to −3% rents per −1pp**, central ~0.5–2% | Barcelona +1.9% avg / +7% top decile; Madrid ~+4% total; Portugal: rents n.s.; NYC removal: ≈0 measured |
| Price effect per +1pp VUT share removed | **−2% to −4%** (up to −8/−9% total in banned saturated zones; −20% for 2-bed segment) | Franco & Santos +3.7%/pp; Lisbon ban −8.7%; Barcelona +5.3%/+17% |
| Asymmetry: removal < arrival effect | removal effect 0–70% of arrival effect | NYC ≈0 vs Berlin 2016 positive; hysteresis via temporada/dark units |
| Conversion of delisted VUT to long-term stock | **10–50%** (NYC: low, unquantified "many did not return"; Berlin 2016: substantial for commercial hosts; German caps: "few") | keep as parameter range, never a point |
| Evasion / substitution share (to temporada or illegal) | **10–50%** of nominally removed units; up to ~90% non-registration in weak-enforcement Madrid | Barcelona 71%-of-illegals-as-temporada; Madrid registry gap; German non-compliance >1/3 |
| License capitalisation into price | 8–9% (quasi-experimental, Lisbon) to 25–35% (Barcelona broker claims, unverified) | removed value on phase-out |

→ see Update 2026-09-08 (KB refresh) at the end of this note.

## 5. Model mapping

Proposed `scenario.Intervention` parameters (typed dataclass; all ranges, seeded draws):

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class TouristRentalRestriction:
    # Cap on VUT licenses as share of zone dwelling stock; None = no cap.
    vut_license_cap: dict[ZoneType, float | None]
    #  e.g. {TENSIONED_METRO: 0.0 (no new), SECONDARY: 0.02, RURAL: None}
    # Fraction of existing licenses extinguished per year (Barcelona step
    # phase-out ≈ 1.0 in the 2028 tick; moratoria = 0 new, 0 phase-out).
    vut_phaseout_rate: dict[ZoneType, float]  # /year, 0.0–1.0
    # Share of extinguished VUT that re-enter the long-term market.
    conversion_to_longterm_share: tuple[float, float] = (0.10, 0.50)
    # Share of nominally restricted units that evade (temporada or illegal),
    # decreasing in enforcement_intensity.
    evasion_share: tuple[float, float] = (0.10, 0.50)
    # Optional: registry enforcement scaling evasion (Madrid ≈ low, BCN ≈ high).
    enforcement_intensity: dict[ZoneType, float]  # 0–1
```

- Rent/price effects must **emerge** from the supply shift, not be imposed; use §4 as
  calibration/validation targets: a synthetic Airbnb-arrival episode in the tensioned-metro
  zone should reproduce rents +1.9% (avg) to +7% (hotspot) per Garcia-López et al., and a
  full phase-out should produce a price drop bounded by the Lisbon −8/−9%.
- Landlord agents should hold a **VUT option value** (8–35% of price, range) that the
  phase-out destroys — this drives sell-vs-convert-vs-evade decisions and reproduces the
  pre-ban registration rush observed in Lisbon.
- Non-converted, non-evading units may exit to owner-occupancy or vacancy ("dark" units,
  NYC pattern): residual share = 1 − conversion − evasion.
- Zone mapping: tensioned metro ← Barcelona/Madrid centre evidence; secondary city ←
  small effects (VUT share <2% → ≈0); rural/coastal ← Canarias/Baleares caps (interaction
  with migration of tourism demand between zones).
