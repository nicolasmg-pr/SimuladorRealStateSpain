# Vacancy tax (IBI surcharge on empty homes)

Researched 2026-08-07. Sources registered per `docs/sources.md` conventions; full rows in
scratch `sources/policy-vacancy-tax.md`. All effect estimates kept as ranges.

## 1. Mechanism

- **State lever: IBI surcharge (art. 72.4 TRLHL, rewritten by Ley 12/2023 DF 3ª, in force
  26-05-2023; verified against BOE consolidated text + gtt.es legal analysis).**
  Municipalities *may* (never must) levy a surcharge on the net IBI quota of dwellings
  "permanently unoccupied":
  - **Base: up to 50%** — dwelling empty continuously **>2 years** without justified cause
    **and owner holds 4+ residential properties** (anywhere, not just in the municipality).
  - **Up to 100%** when vacancy exceeds **3 years** (modulable by duration).
  - **+50 pp additional** if the owner has **2+ empty dwellings in the same municipality**
    → statutory ceiling **150%** of the IBI quota.
  - Statutorily justified causes (no surcharge): temporary work/study relocation,
    dependency/health/social emergency, **second homes up to 4 years' continuous vacancy**,
    works/rehabilitation, litigation, dwelling actively offered for sale (≤1 yr) or rent
    (≤6 months). The second-home carve-out alone shields most of the "empty" census stock.
  - Procedure: the municipality must write requirements, evidence and procedure into its
    fiscal ordinance, then issue a **per-dwelling administrative declaration of vacancy**
    with prior hearing of the owner, re-verified **each year** at the 31-Dec accrual
    (evidence: padrón data, utility consumption). CEMCI evaluation (Andreu Zamorano, 2025):
    pre-2023 the concept was legally undefined and regional high courts (Madrid, Andalucía,
    Asturias, Cataluña, Galicia STSJs 2010–2016) annulled municipal ordinances that tried
    to define it — hence near-zero effective application before Ley 12/2023.
- **Order of magnitude of the levy.** IBI urban rates are 0.4–1.1% of cadastral value,
  cadastral value typically well below market. A 50–150% surcharge is therefore roughly
  **0.1–0.5% of market value per year** — an order of magnitude weaker than Vancouver's 3%
  of assessed value.
- **Catalonia's separate regional tax (Ley 14/2015, impuesto sobre las viviendas vacías).**
  Own regional tax on dwellings empty **>2 years** without cause, originally on **legal
  persons only** (banks, real-estate firms), extended to securitisation funds (DL 12/2019)
  and, per the ATC's current page (2026), to natural-person *grandes tenedores*. Applies in
  **72 high-demand municipalities**; scale **€10–30/m²/yr** by portfolio size (≈ €850–1,650
  per dwelling per year); **bonuses up to 100%** of the quota for owners putting stock into
  affordable rental; revenue earmarked for housing plans, prioritising the collecting
  municipalities. Upheld by Tribunal Constitucional (STC 4/2019) against the state's
  IBI-overlap challenge, and by Tribunal Supremo (2022) against the banking association
  (AEB) appeal.
- **Basque Country.** Ley 3/2015 (arts. 57–58) + Decreto 149/2021: regional *canon* of
  **€10/m²/yr on dwellings declared vacant >2 years**, rising 10%/yr up to 3× the initial
  amount, tied to a formal vacancy declaration and registry (Etxebide); proceeds go to
  municipalities for housing. In Gipuzkoa the foral IBI surcharge route is the one actually
  used (see uptake below).
- **Navarra.** The only *mandatory* design: the Impuesto sobre Viviendas Deshabitadas (Ley
  Foral 2/1995 arts. 132/184 ss., made obligatory by Ley Foral 31/2013) taxes dwellings
  entered in the regional **Registro de Viviendas Deshabitadas** at **0.1–0.5% (yr 1),
  0.5–1% (yr 2), 1–1.5% (yr 3+)** of the contribución territorial base. In practice it has
  barely operated: the FNMC only circulated a model ordinance in **March 2026**, after the
  Parlamento demanded the registry be made usable — three decades after enactment.
- **The definitional bottleneck.** There is **no reliable vacancy registry in Spain**. The
  Censo 2021 counted **3.8M "empty" dwellings (14.4% of stock)** using an electricity-use
  proxy: no supply contract, or annual consumption below the municipality-specific
  equivalent of 15 days' occupation, measured on **2020 (lockdown-year) consumption**, and
  published as provisional, not linked to identifiable dwellings (INE; OVV Euskadi review).
  Critiques (Julio Rodríguez López / Economistas Frente a la Crisis; OVV): not comparable
  with the interviewer-based 2011 census; pandemic-year false positives; the category sits
  inside 8.1M non-principal dwellings and shades into second/seasonal homes; census
  "vacancy" ≠ legally taxable vacancy (which excludes second homes <4 yrs, dwellings on
  the market, litigation, etc.). Municipalities must rebuild the evidence dwelling by
  dwelling from padrón + utility data.

## 2. Evidence for

- **France, TLV (Segú 2020, Journal of Public Economics — the cleanest quasi-experiment).**
  The 1999 taxe sur les logements vacants (now 17% of cadastral rental value in year 1, 34%
  after) **reduced vacancy ~13%** in taxed municipalities vs matched controls, concentrated
  in **long-term** vacancy, with most units returning to primary-residence use. Shows a
  recurring vacancy levy does mobilise stock where demand exists.
- **Vancouver Empty Homes Tax (1% of assessed value 2017 → 3% since 2021).**
  - Official annual reports (City of Vancouver, Nov 2025): declared/deemed vacant
    properties **−67% from 2017 to 2024** (to 979 homes); point-in-time vacancy rate
    **0.90% → 0.49%**. Government self-evaluation — declaration-based.
  - Independent DiD (Caracciolo & Miglino, C.D. Howe Institute, census data at the
    Vancouver–Burnaby border): vacancy rate **−1.5 pp ≈ −21% of empty homes** (~226 homes
    occupied); **no significant effect on rents** or on new construction.
  - So the −67% headline and the −21% causal estimate bracket the truth; both far above
    France's 13% — consistent with a much higher tax rate.
- **Catalonia (the only Spanish quantitative signal).** Vacant dwellings held by banks and
  real-estate firms fell **from ~27,500 (2016) to ~17,500 (2018), −36%** (transparency-law
  data via El Confidencial); total large-holder stock −17% over the same window. Revenue:
  **€11.5M in 2016 per ATC accounts** (El Nacional) vs **€18–19M for 2016** cited by the
  conseller and El Confidencial (base/net discrepancy unresolved — kept as range);
  **€66.8M net cumulative 2016–2020** (Público). Only ~8,300 of the 17,500 vacant units
  actually paid in 2018, ~80% owned by three entities — the tax is effectively a levy on a
  handful of financial portfolios.
- **Uptake grew where rental stress grew.** Gipuzkoa: **54 of 88 municipalities** charge a
  foral IBI vacancy surcharge in 2025 (61%), 14 raised it in 2024–25, five now at the 150%
  ceiling incl. San Sebastián, Irun, Errenteria (El Diario Vasco, 2025-11-24). Cádiz
  adopted a 50→100%+50pp ordinance in Oct 2025. Several large capitals (Barcelona,
  Zaragoza, Sevilla, A Coruña, Córdoba…) have ordinances — though Madrid notably still
  does not; Barcelona's (art. 10, IBI ordinance 2025) is cited by CEMCI as the model
  implementation.
- **Fiscal upside is real, if small.** Revenue is positive net of collection where
  registries exist (Catalonia's €12–19M/yr); the earmarking to housing policy converts the
  tax into a funding channel even when mobilization is weak.

## 3. Evidence against / side effects

- **Uptake is the story: almost nobody applies it.** Ministerio de Hacienda ordinance data
  (via ABC, 2023-05-25): **222 of 8,131 municipalities** had the IBI surcharge regulated in
  2022 — versus 175 in 2008 and 215 in 2018, i.e. essentially flat for 15 years; only ~10
  provincial capitals; **one third of adopters in Barcelona province**; the vacant homes in
  adopting municipalities are **<10% of Spain's census vacant stock**. Madrid still has no
  surcharge in its ordinance (CEMCI, 2025).
- **Geographic mismatch: rural vacancy dominates, tensioned zones don't.** Censo 2021:
  municipalities **<10,000 inhabitants hold 45.0% of all empty homes** with 20.3% of
  population; cities **>250,000 hold only 10.5%** (38.3 vs 3.6 empty dwellings per 100
  residents). Madrid region 6.3% vacancy, País Vasco 6.5% vs Galicia 28.8%. Most of the
  3.8M headline stock is where nobody wants to rent, and much of it is uninhabitable or
  seasonal — the mobilisable stock in tensioned zones is a small fraction of the headline.
- **Definitional evasion and nominal occupation.** Owners can defeat detection cheaply:
  register a relative in the padrón, keep a minimum electricity contract, list the unit
  "for sale/rent" at an off-market price (the statutory market-offer carve-out), or invoke
  the 4-year second-home exemption. Vancouver's own evaluations rely on unverifiable
  self-declarations (C.D. Howe caveat); the Cantabria model ordinance shows how many
  documented escape routes exist.
- **Enforcement cost per euro raised is high.** Annual per-dwelling declaration with prior
  hearing, evidence-gathering and litigation risk (gtt.es; CEMCI). Small municipalities —
  where the vacant stock is — lack the staff (CEMCI). Navarra's mandatory tax produced no
  effective registry for ~30 years. **Legazpi (Gipuzkoa) repealed its 50% surcharge in 2025
  after ten years: "it has not served to bring empty homes to market" (mayor, PNV)** — a
  direct adopter-side negative evaluation.
- **Narrow statutory base.** Fotocasa Research (2023, industry viewpoint): only **~5% of
  individual owners of empty dwellings** would even qualify for the surcharge under the
  4-property/2-year/no-cause test; punitive measures "will not massively expand supply".
- **Attribution is weak even in the Catalan success case.** García Montalvo (in Fotocasa,
  2015): holding costs of bank stock were already ~14%/yr, so an €850–1,650 levy changes
  little; the 2016–2018 fall in bank-held vacancy coincided with Sareb/servicer portfolio
  sales and a booming market. Experts consulted by El Confidencial (2019): data
  insufficient to attribute the −36% to the tax.
- **Theoretical distortion (Han, Stacey & Chen 2023, ABFER working paper, Vancouver).**
  A vacancy tax cuts *speculative* vacancies but also taxes *frictional* vacancies (homes
  empty while being sold), distorting listing incentives — in their calibrated model this
  **raises house prices and lowers home-ownership** even as rental availability improves.
  Empirically C.D. Howe finds **no rent decline** in Vancouver: mobilization ≠ measured
  price relief, at least at city scale over 5 years.

## 3b. Where the empty stock actually is (Funcas 104 ch.1, INE Censo 2021)

Added 2026-08-14. This is the strongest constraint on what the lever can achieve, and it is
geographic, not behavioural.

- Empty dwellings as a share of the local park: **24.6%** in municipalities under 5,000
  inhabitants, 15.6% at 10–20k, 13.1% at 20–40k, ≈11.5% at 40–150k, ≈8% at 150–500k, **6.3%**
  in Madrid. National 13.2% of a 24.96M park (3.29M dwellings; the chapter text cites 3.8M on
  a wider definition).
- Municipalities under 20,000 inhabitants hold **50% of the empty stock** with 28% of the
  population. Provinces whose household count grew slower than the 3.1% national rate hold
  **>60%** of it; the fastest-growing provinces (>4.7%) hold **5%**.
- The chapter's own conclusion: this stock "can hardly serve as an umbrella" for unmet demand
  — wrong location, and much of it needing substantial rehabilitation.

Consequences for this lever, both already in the model (`ZoneConfig.units_per_household`,
`ZoneConfig.withheld_share`, see `docs/funcas-104.md`): a national vacancy tax mobilises stock
where demand is weakest, so the units it frees are not the units the tensioned market needs;
and a tax whose bite is calibrated on tensioned-zone vacancy is calibrated on the 6–7% tail of
the distribution, not on the 20%+ mass. The Ley 12/2023 instrument itself is confirmed by
ch.7: a **50–150% IBI surcharge** on owners of four or more residential dwellings empty ≥2
years without justification, i.e. ≈0.1–0.8% of market value per year at typical IBI rates —
the low half of this model's `vacancy_tax_rate` range.

## 4. Effect-size range

All ranges, never points; Spanish causal evidence is absent — international estimates
imported, a stated model limitation.

- **Vacancy reduction among the taxed/targeted stock:** **−13%** (France, ~0.2–0.5%-of-value
  tax; Segú 2020) to **−21%…−67%** (Vancouver, 1→3%-of-value tax; C.D. Howe causal vs city
  cumulative declared). Catalonia large-holder stock: **−17% to −36%** over 2016–2018 with
  attribution disputed (tax vs portfolio sales). Effect scales with the effective rate and
  is concentrated in long-term vacancy.
- **Share of Spanish census "vacant" stock actually reachable:** adopting municipalities
  contain **<10%** of census vacant homes (2022); of owners of empty homes only **~5%**
  meet the statutory test; second-home/for-sale carve-outs shrink it further. Effective
  national mobilization potential of the current IBI instrument: **well under 1–2% of the
  3.8M headline**, concentrated in tensioned metros.
- **Effect on rents/prices:** Vancouver DiD — **no statistically significant rent effect**;
  theory (Han et al.) admits **small positive price effects** via listing distortion; no
  credible Spanish estimate exists. Treat rent/price impact as flowing endogenously from
  the mobilized-supply shock, not as a direct parameter.
- **Revenue (Catalonia benchmark):** €11.5–19M/yr on ~8–17k taxed dwellings in 72
  municipalities; declining as the base shrinks (finalist design).

## 5. Model mapping

Proposed `scenario.Intervention` parameters (typed dataclass; all ranges to be swept, seeds
fixed; disputed values stay ranges per bias-control rule):

- `vacancy_tax_rate: float` — annual tax as a **fraction of dwelling market value** on
  units flagged vacant. Spanish IBI surcharge regime: **0.001–0.005** (50–150% of an IBI
  quota of 0.4–1.1% on below-market cadastral values). Catalan/Basque regional taxes:
  **0.003–0.01**. Vancouver-style upper scenario: **0.01–0.03**. Sweeping this range spans
  every observed design.
- `detection_probability: float` — annual probability that a truly-vacant, statutorily
  liable dwelling is declared and billed. Captures municipal uptake + per-dwelling
  procedure + evasion (padrón registration, minimal electricity, sham listings).
  Baseline Spain: **0.0–0.05** (222/8,131 municipalities, <10% of vacant stock covered);
  committed-enforcement scenario (Gipuzkoa/Catalonia registry): **0.1–0.3**;
  Vancouver-style universal declaration: **0.5–0.9**.
- `applies_to_zone: set[ZoneType]` — default `{tensioned_metro}` (Catalonia's 72-municipality
  model; adoption is concentrated there). Applying it to `rural` should show ~no effect —
  a useful validation check, since 45% of census vacancy is in <10k municipalities where
  the lever is neither adopted nor meaningful.
- `min_portfolio_size: int` — statutory scope: **4** (state IBI regime) or large-holder
  thresholds (Catalonia ≥15/legal persons). Only landlord/investor agents with holdings ≥
  this are liable; households with one empty flat are out of scope, matching Fotocasa's
  ~5% figure.
- `min_vacancy_duration: int` — ticks equivalent to **2 years** before liability (3 years
  for the higher band if rate is modelled as tiered).
- `mobilization_elasticity: float` — annual probability that a *detected and taxed* vacant
  unit is returned to the rental/sale market, as a function of tax burden. Derived from the
  observed −13% to −36% (to −67% upper) cumulative reductions over ~2–4 years:
  **0.04–0.15 per year** at Spanish-level rates, up to **0.15–0.30** at Vancouver-level
  rates. Keep as a range; the France/Vancouver disagreement is a rate effect, not a
  resolvable dispute.
- Interaction to preserve: mobilized units add to *rental* supply first (France: mostly
  primary-residence conversion; Catalonia: affordable-rental bonus design), and the model
  should not hard-code any rent decline — C.D. Howe found none; let clearing decide.
