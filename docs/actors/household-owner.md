# Household — owner-occupier & aspiring buyer

Status: **researched dossier** (fetched 2026-08-07). Latest data wave: EFF2024 (Banco de
España, Documento Ocasional 2610, published 2026-04-16; income refers to calendar year
2023, balance sheets to end-2024) plus BdE Síntesis de Indicadores 1.5 (data through
Q2 2026). Source register: `docs/sources.md` + scratch sources file for this dossier.

## 1. Role & size in the market

- **70.6% of Spanish households own their main residence** (end-2024, EFF2024), down from
  72.1% in 2022 — a slow structural decline from ~83% in the 2000s. Ownership is the
  default tenure and the dominant household wealth vehicle: residential wealth of
  households ≈ 726% of gross disposable income (2025, BdE), vs net financial wealth ≈ 249%.
- Spain had **19.85M households in 2025** (EPA), growing to 20.0M by Q2 2026. Net household
  formation is the primary demand driver: observed +136k (2024) to +258k (2025) y/y per
  EPA quarters; 2020–2024 average ≈ 239.5k/yr (Economistas Frente a la Crisis, from INE);
  INE projection 2026–2041 averages ≈ 205k/yr in 2026–2031, decaying to ≈ 93k/yr in
  2036–2041 (this projection *cut* the previous 2024–2039 one, which had 246k/yr).
  The claim "≈240k/yr recently" is verified but sits inside a wide 135k–260k observed band.
- Transaction flow (2025): **714k registered sales (INE)** / 756k per MIVAU — highest
  since 2008. Against ~19.9M households that is ≈ 3.6% of households transacting per year
  (≈2.7% of the ~26M dwelling stock).
- **Foreign buyers are a significant demand overlay**: 13.8% of 2025 purchases
  (~97,300 units, Registradores) vs ~18% per MIVAU/Notariado counts (18.0% in the year to
  Q1 2025; 18.4% in 2S 2025). The 14–17% prior is confirmed as a between-sources range,
  not a point. Split ≈ 56% resident foreigners (behave like Spanish households: urban,
  financed, similar prices) / 44% non-resident (holiday/investment, coastal-island,
  high budgets). Concentration: Alicante 45.7%, Baleares 32.8%, Málaga 32.3%,
  S.C. Tenerife 30.7%, Girona 28.9% of all local sales (Q4 2025, Registradores).
- Generational shift: home-ownership at age 42 was 67% for the 1975–85 cohort vs >81% for
  1945–65 cohorts (EFF 20-year analysis); ownership among 16–29-year-olds fell from ~58%
  (2007) to ~29% (2023) (Fotocasa Research). Average age at first purchase now ≈ 41
  (Fotocasa Research 2023/2026), ~7 years above the EU average.

## 2. Balance sheet & constraints

EFF2024 (medians unless noted; € of 2023/2024):

- **Income**: median gross household income 36,100 €/yr (mean 46,300). By age of head:
  <35: 32,000; 35–44: 39,100; 55–64: 40,400 (peak); >74: 24,400. Owners 41,100 vs
  non-owners 25,900 — owners are structurally richer.
- **Net wealth**: median 160,800 € (mean 344,700). By age: <35: 22,900 (mean 90,100);
  35–44: 80,200; 65–74: 249,200 (peak). The <35 median cannot fund a typical down payment
  without family help — the central constraint on entry.
- **Main residence**: median self-reported value 170,000 € (owners). 45.3% of households
  also own other real estate (33.7% another dwelling) — owner and small-investor roles
  blur.
- **Debt**: 54% of households carry some debt. **25% have main-residence purchase debt**
  (= 35.4% of owner-occupiers), median outstanding 60,900 € (down from 68,400 in 2022);
  almost all mortgage-secured. Incidence peaks at ages 45–54 (40.1%).
- **Debt service**: median debt-payments/gross-income of indebted households 13.4%;
  share with ratio >40% ("distressed") = 7.9% (was 8.5% in 2022; 25.5% in the bottom
  income quintile). Median debt-stock/income of indebted households 69.7% (103.2% for
  ages 35–44).
- **Purchase-side constraints** (BdE Síntesis 1.5, 2023 → 2025 → latest 2026):
  - Average LTV of new mortgages 62.3% → 64.8% → 63.7% (Q2 26); IEF reports ~67.2% (2024)
    on a slightly different basis. Share of new loans with LTV >80%: 6.5% → 11.3% → 9.6%.
    The effective bank norm is **max 80% LTV**, i.e. ≥20% down payment plus 10–12%
    taxes/costs → **~30–32% of price needed upfront**.
  - Average new-mortgage term 24.5 → 25.6 years; rate 3.9% (2023) → 2.7–2.9% (2025–26).
  - **Price-to-income**: house price / gross disposable income per household 7.1 (2024)
    → 7.6 (2025) → 8.0 years (Q1 2026) — first time at 8 since 2011.
  - **Theoretical effort** (first-year payments of an 80%-LTV loan on a typical dwelling
    / median household disposable income): 34.3% (2025) → 36.1% (Q1 2026), around BdE's
    35% risk reference.
  - Average new mortgage principal 172,535 € (2025, INE), +13.5% y/y → implied
    loan-to-income ≈ 3–4× gross income for the buying population (derived).
- **Cash buyers**: ~30% of 2025 registered purchases had no mortgage attached (INE-based
  counts; higher shares circulate on other bases). Cash weight rises with the
  foreign-non-resident and investor overlay.
- Saving flow: household saving rate stayed "historically elevated" post-2023 (EFF2024
  narrative); national-accounts rate ≈ 13–14% of disposable income in 2024–25 (unverified
  point value). Young households need ≈ 3.6 years of saving for a 20% down payment vs
  2.6 years in 1993 (idealista/Infobae, Tier 2, 2026).

## 3. Observed decision rules (with evidence)

- **Buy vs rent is affordability-gated, not preference-gated.** Ownership preference
  stays high across surveys, but entry requires simultaneously (a) ≥20% + costs in liquid
  savings, (b) debt service ≤ ~35% of income under the bank's test. Evidence: down-payment
  wall (EFF: <35 median net wealth 22.9k vs ~50–55k needed on a median dwelling), effort
  ratio hovering at the 35% threshold (BdE), and the jump in young-household ownership
  (+4.8pp, first rise since 2011, EFF2024) exactly when income grew (+6.2% median <35),
  rates fell, and the 2024–25 state guarantee (ICO aval 20%, enabling up to 100%
  financing for first-time buyers) relaxed the down-payment constraint. Two independent
  levers (income/rates vs guarantee) moved together — the model should treat both.
- **Willingness-to-pay formation**: anchored on (i) bank-permitted budget
  (income × DSTI cap ÷ annuity factor + savings) and (ii) recent observed price growth —
  extrapolative expectations. Direct Spanish survey evidence on expectation elasticity is
  thin (open question); the 2021–2025 episode (transactions and prices rising together
  while affordability worsened) is consistent with momentum/FOMO behaviour, as is the
  2006–07 episode. Treat expectation formation as adaptive with a momentum term
  (parameter below, confidence low).
- **Triggers to buy/move**: household formation (couple formation, emancipation —
  average first purchase at ~41; buyers concentrated at 35–44 (32%) and 25–34 (30%),
  Fotocasa); job/city change (migration between zones); household enlargement. Mobility
  is low: ≈3.6% of households transact per year, and most owner households never re-move
  (ownership >80% and stable above age 65, EFF).
- **Family transfers matter**: young-household entry co-moves with parental wealth
  (EFF cohort analysis shows ownership gaps by parental cohort wealth); treat a share of
  down payments as family-funded (parameter kept as a range, weak sourcing).
- **Foreign non-resident rule set differs**: buys for holiday/investment in coastal/island
  zones, pays 76–79% above the national average €/m² (3,063 vs 1,713 €/m² 2S 2024;
  3,242 vs 1,839 €/m² 2S 2025; Notariado via CaixaBank Research), largely un-mortgaged
  (cash share high but unverified as a point), and is sensitive to origin-country
  conditions more than Spanish rates: non-resident purchases fell 15.1% y/y in 2S 2025
  while resident-foreigner purchases rose 3.3%. Resident foreigners should be modelled as
  ordinary households, not as the overlay.

## 4. Reaction to past shocks (case episodes)

- **2008–2013 bust**: prices fell ~12% by 2009 vs 2007 (BdE via secondary source);
  cumulative decline to 2013 reported up to 45% (real terms / valuation sources) —
  nominal INE/MIVAU peak-to-trough commonly cited at ~30–37% (unverified exact figure;
  record as range 30–45% depending on deflator/source). New home-purchase mortgages
  collapsed to 274,715 in 2012, the lowest since the bubble burst; transaction volumes
  roughly a third of the 2006 peak (~955k in 2006, unverified). Households responded by
  freezing purchases for years, not by mass distressed selling — sales volume, not price,
  absorbed most of the initial shock; prices then ground down over 6 years. Young
  ownership entry fell continuously from 2011 to 2022 (EFF).
- **2013–2019 recovery**: median incomes grew 2016–2019 across all but the lowest
  quintile (EFF); transactions and prices recovered gradually; foreign share of purchases
  rose from 2.9% of sales (2007, non-residents) to a ~15% average of all foreigners
  2006–2024 (CaixaBank/Registradores).
- **2022–23 rate shock**: new-mortgage rates 1.5%→3.9%; sales fell **–11% in 2023**
  (MIVAU) and mortgages ~–18%, but **prices did not fall** (IPV +4.0% in 2023) — demand
  volume adjusted, prices stayed sticky, and both stayed above 2019 levels (EFF2024
  narrative). Indebted households' distress barely moved (share with debt service >40%
  fell 8.5%→7.9% by 2024) because most stock had amortised and employment held.
- **2021–2025 run-up**: IPV +8.4% (2024), **+12.7% (2025 average; verified — quarterly
  peak 12.9% in Q4 2025/Q1 2026, the largest rise in 18 years)**, driven by household
  formation + migration (population +~600–740k/yr, 19.3% foreign-born by 2025) against
  completions covering <50–70% of household formation. Buyers responded to the 2024–25
  rate decline (2.7–2.8%) with a demand surge (sales +10.7% to a 17-year high) despite
  worsening price-to-income — evidence that the credit-access constraint binds more than
  the price level. Early 2026 shows volume cooling (Q1 2026 sales –11% y/y) with prices
  still rising ~13%: the same "volume adjusts first, prices are sticky" signature as 2022.

## 5. Power & relations to other actors

- **vs Bank**: price-taker on rates and underwriting; the bank's LTV/DSTI caps define the
  household's budget line. Household leverage is the bank's main retail asset (mortgage
  stock) — the bank actor's credit-standard parameter directly gates this actor's entry.
- **vs Landlord / tenant role**: aspiring buyers rent while queuing; rent inflation
  simultaneously raises the incentive to buy and destroys saving capacity (double-edged —
  model both channels). 45.3% of owner households also hold other property, so
  "household-owner" overlaps with `investor-small`.
- **vs Developer**: buys new stock (new dwellings ≈ 20% of registered sales 2025);
  aspiring buyers absorb whatever completions exist — the completions/formation gap
  (~50–70%) is set by the developer actor.
- **vs Government**: recipient of demand-side supports (ICO 20% guarantee for
  first-time/young buyers enabling 100% financing; regional 15–20% guarantees; buyer pays
  ITP 6–10% or VAT 10% + costs ≈ 10–12% of price). Demand subsidies relax the
  down-payment constraint without adding supply — a key policy experiment.
- **vs Foreign non-resident buyers**: compete directly in tensioned coastal/island zones
  (up to 46% of local sales), raising the local price anchor; negligible competition in
  most inland secondary/rural zones.

## 7. Open questions

1. **Expectation elasticity**: no Spanish micro-estimate found of how much WTP moves per
   point of observed price growth. Candidate sources: BdE working papers on expectations,
   ECB Consumer Expectations Survey (Spain sample). Until then the momentum parameter is
   a free calibration knob — validate against the 2022–23 episode (volume −11%, prices +4%).
2. **Family transfers**: share and size of parental help in first purchases — EFF has
   inheritance/gift variables (microdata exploitation needed); current row is a guess.
3. **Cash-purchase composition**: how much of the ~30% mortgage-free share is foreign
   non-residents vs domestic equity movers (sellers re-buying) vs investors? Notariado
   microdata could split it; matters for rate-sensitivity of demand.
4. **Foreign-share methodology gap** (13.8% Registradores vs 18.4% Notariado/MIVAU):
   understand base differences (registered vs notarised, dwelling definitions) before
   choosing a simulation anchor; currently modelled as a range.
5. **Household-formation projection risk**: INE cut its projection by ~40k/yr in the 2026
   revision (246k→205k); migration assumptions dominate. Formation rate should be a
   scenario input, not a constant.
6. **Down-payment saving behaviour**: no direct series on saving *rate of aspiring
   buyers* (vs all households); EFF microdata by tenure and age could estimate it.
7. **Selling/moving triggers of existing owners** (divorce, death, job moves): turnover
   ≈3.6%/yr is aggregate; a split by motive would let the model distinguish forced from
   opportunistic listings.
8. **2008 bust magnitudes**: pin exact INE/MIVAU nominal peak-to-trough and the 2013
   transaction trough from the primary series (currently a 30–45% range from mixed
   deflators; secondary-sourced).
