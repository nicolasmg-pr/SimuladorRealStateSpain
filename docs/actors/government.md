Convention: **Tier 1** = legal texts / official statistics / court rulings; **Tier 2** = interpretive

## 1. Role & size in the market

The Spanish "government" is not one actor. Housing competence is **exclusively regional**
(art. 148.1.3ª CE); the state legislates through side doors (equal exercise of rights art. 149.1.1ª,
economic planning 149.1.13ª, civil legislation 149.1.8ª, taxation), and municipalities hold the
property tax (IBI) and land-use/licensing power. Every national lever therefore has a regional or
municipal veto point, and the Constitutional Court (TC) and Supreme Court (TS) actively police the
boundary — several state instruments have already been trimmed (see §4).

As a direct market actor the public sector is small:

- Public social-rental stock: **≈318,000 dwellings** (≈197k CCAA-owned, ≈121k municipal),
  ≈ **1.5–3.3 % of stock** depending on definition/denominator, vs **≈8–9.3 % EU average**
  (Provivienda Observatorio using MIVAU 2024 count; triangulated against press/Wikipedia citing
  MIVAU and BdE).
- Direct public spending on housing: ≈ **0.1 % of GDP** (2023) vs ≈ **1.05 % eurozone average**
  (Habitaro/Provivienda citing Eurostat; Eurostat series itself not fetched — treat as range
  0.1–0.5 % once tax expenditures are included **(upper bound unverified)**).
- Announced scale-up: **Plan Estatal de Vivienda 2026–2030 = €7 bn / 5 yr** (€4.2 bn state /
  €2.8 bn CCAA; 60/40 split from 2027) — roughly triple the previous plan, still only ≈0.09 %
  GDP/yr all-in (RD 326/2026, BOE-A-2026-8872; La Moncloa 17-09-2025).
- New public operator: **Sepes → state housing & land company**, receiving from Sareb **≈40,000
  dwellings + ≈2,400 land plots (capacity ≈55,000 homes), value ≈€5.9 bn**, plus **€593 M** to
  refurbish/develop; homes go to permanently-protected affordable rental (Council of Ministers /
  BOE order 07-2025; MIVAU transfer page; idealista/elEconomista).

Modelling note: the state's *regulatory* footprint (rent caps, tax wedges) is far larger than its
*ownership* footprint. In the ABM the government mostly changes other actors' payoffs; its direct
supply pipeline (~40–95k units over years) is small against ~19M primary residences.

## 2. Balance sheet & constraints

- **Fiscal**: state housing budget historically ≈€1–2 bn/yr; Plan 2026–2030 lifts state commitment
  to ≈€0.84 bn/yr plus CCAA co-funding. ICO purchase-guarantee line: up to **€2.5 bn contingent
  liabilities** (guarantees, not outlays). Sepes received assets, not cash: €5.9 bn in
  dwellings/land plus a €593 M investment envelope.
- **Legal/constitutional**: the state cannot impose rent regulation on CCAA that refuse to declare
  tensioned zones; the TC annuls overreach (STC 79/2024 annulled parts of the state VPO regime —
  arts. 16, 19.3 2nd clause, 27.1 3rd para, 27.3, DT 1ª — as invasion of CCAA competence;
  STC 26/2025 annulled the gran-tenedor eviction pre-conditions in LEC arts. 439.6.c/439.7 and
  655 bis 1–2; TS sentences 620/2026 etc. annulled the state single short-term-rental registry).
  EU free movement of capital constrains discrimination against foreign buyers (relevant to the
  100 % non-EU tax proposal — The Conversation / Serveco legal analyses).
- **Political**: minority national government since 2023 — flagship tax measures (100 % non-EU tax,
  SOCIMI reform) sit in parliamentary limbo. Opposition-run CCAA (Madrid, Andalucía, Valencia…)
  refuse to activate Ley 12/2023 instruments and litigate instead (recursos behind STC 79/2024,
  17/2026).
- **Administrative**: systematic lag between announcement and delivery (Jan-2025 12-measure plan:
  golden-visa end delivered 04-2025; 100 % tax and SOCIMI reform still pending mid-2026).

## 3. Observed decision rules (with evidence)

1. **State legislates framework + fiscal carrots; uptake follows partisan alignment.** Only
   left/nationalist-governed CCAA (Catalonia, Euskadi, Navarra, Asturias) declared tensioned zones
   2024–26; PP-run Madrid and Andalucía refused and litigated. Nuance: PP-run **Galicia processed
   municipal requests** (A Coruña 07-2025, Santiago 07-2026) — municipal demand can override
   regional partisanship (Civio BOE tracker; EjePrime).
2. **Reactive, announcement-heavy policymaking.** Major packages follow rent surges and electoral
   windows (Ley 12/2023 weeks before May-2023 elections; Jan-2025 plan after 2024 rent records).
   Model as: intervention probability rising with rent-growth / affordability-stress indicators.
3. **Preference for demand-side and off-budget instruments** (ICO guarantees, IRPF reductions,
   Bono Alquiler Joven) over direct building — consistent with 0.1 % GDP direct spend. BdE/Fedea
   have publicly criticised demand subsidies as capitalising into prices **(positions widely
   reported; the specific reports were not fetched — unverified)**.
4. **Municipalities restrict tourist use where tourism pressure is high**: Barcelona total licence
   freeze + announced non-renewal of ≈10,100 licences by 11-2028; Madrid Plan RESIDE (2025)
   confines tourist flats largely to tertiary-use buildings (Lodgify/Chekin regulatory guides,
   consistent across both).
5. **Courts act as a recurring veto player**: each new instrument carries ≈1–3 yr litigation risk;
   the model should distinguish "policy adopted" from "policy surviving review" (five TC sentences
   on Ley 12/2023 alone: 79/2024, 26/2025, 190/2025, 17/2026, 41/2026 — BOE).

## 4. Reaction to past shocks (case episodes)

- **2008–2013 bust**: bank rescue; **Sareb** created 2012 to absorb distressed real-estate assets;
  VPO production collapsed from >60k/yr to ~10k/yr **(order of magnitude; official series not
  fetched — unverified)**; Madrid-region public-housing sales to funds (2013) **(unverified)**.
  Net effect: the public stock shrank exactly when demand for it rose.
- **COVID-2020**: eviction moratoria for vulnerable households (RDL 11/2020, repeatedly extended;
  current end-date **(unverified)**); no lasting supply response.
- **2022 inflation shock**: emergency **rent-update caps 2 % (2022–23), 3 % (2024)**, then
  permanent replacement of CPI by the **IRAV** index from 01-2025 for contracts signed after
  26-05-2023 (cap 2.20 % in 2025; readings 2.40–2.44 % mid-2026) (Ley 12/2023; INE IRAV via
  Fotocasa/lexway/irav.es trackers, mutually consistent).
- **2020–2022 Catalan precedent**: Catalonia's own rent cap (Ley 11/2020) was annulled by the TC
  (STC 37/2022 **(reference from memory — unverified)**) for invading state civil-law competence —
  this is why rent control now runs through state law + CCAA declaration, the reverse veto
  structure.
- **2023–2026 affordability crisis**: Ley 12/2023; golden-visa abolition (03-04-2025); Sareb→Sepes
  transfer (2025); Plan Estatal tripling (2026); short-term-rental single registry (07-2025, core
  annulled by TS 05/06-2026); SOCIMI reform and 100 % non-EU tax proposed but **not enacted** as of
  08-2026.

## 5. Power & relations to other actors

- **State ↔ CCAA**: the state sets the *option set* (tensioned-zone regime, tax incentives,
  indices); CCAA hold the *switch*. Declarations last 3 years, renewable, published via MIVAU after
  CCAA request. CCAA also set ITP/AJD rates (the main transaction tax) and execute VPO programmes.
- **State ↔ municipalities**: municipalities own IBI (incl. the empty-home surcharge switch),
  planning and tourist licensing; the TS confirmed the state cannot displace regional/local
  registries (single-registry annulment, TS 620/2026 and companions).
- **Government ↔ landlords/investors**: the gran tenedor (>10 dwellings or >1,500 m²; ≥5 where a
  CCAA so decides in tensioned zones) is the targeted class: index-capped new rents in tensioned
  zones, 20 % Catalan ITP, planned SOCIMI tightening. Documented evasion channel: shift to
  seasonal/room rentals; counter-regulation is still only attempted (state bill failed; Catalan
  decree status **(unverified)**).
- **Government ↔ banks/households**: the ICO guarantee (20 %, 25 % if energy-efficient) shifts
  mortgage tail risk to the state for buyers ≤35 (or with minor children), enabling ~100 % LTV.
- **Government ↔ EU**: EU capital-movement law constrains anti-foreign-buyer taxation (it did not
  block the golden-visa repeal); Next-Gen funds co-finance rehabilitation lines **(NGEU detail
  unverified)**.
