# Sealed hold-out: the 2008–2013 bust

**Status: sealed 2026-09-11. Runnable from phase C (2026-09-14) — the insolvency chain
exists and both inputs have levers. Still NOT RUN: the hold-out is executed once, in phase E,
after recalibration, and no parameter is touched afterwards.**

## The rule

No parameter of this model may be changed after this episode has been run. The episode is
evidence about the specification, and re-fitting against it converts it into a fitting sample,
which is the one irreparable objection available against the project (`model-spec.md §13.4`).

Concretely:

- Calibration uses **2014–2025 moments only** (`docs/validation.md`).
- This file may gain inputs and sources. It may not gain *results* until phase E.
- When it is run, the result is recorded here once, pass or fail, with the commit hash and
  seeds. A failure is reported as a failure.

## What the episode is

Spain 2008–2013: the credit stop, the volume collapse, the slow price grind and the
foreclosure wave. The model must reproduce it from inputs alone, with no parameter fitted to
it.

The legal switch matters more than it looks: the episode ran under a regime that terminated a
loan after three missed instalments, and the model's default regime is the post-2019 one that
needs twelve. Running the hold-out under today's law would understate the foreclosure wave by
roughly three quarters of arrears per borrower.

## Why it is the right hold-out

It is the episode no parameter in the model has seen, and the only one that exercises the
mechanisms the model lacked until phases C and D: a binding budget constraint, forced sale,
and negative equity locking sellers in — the last of which arrived with phase D's seller
reserve, so all three now exist. Reproducing a boom the model was built during is a much
weaker claim.

Phase D changed one thing that matters for this episode and is worth stating before the run:
the seller's reserve is now the outstanding mortgage, so a household in negative equity cannot
sell at all. In a bust that turns the volume collapse into an accounting consequence rather
than a behavioural assumption, and it is the missing half of the foreclosure prediction below
— the homes that cannot be sold are the ones the lender ends up taking.

What phase C already says about the outcome, on the record before the run: with a calm
baseline the model produces ≈0.02% of mortgages delivered per year against 0.10–0.16%
observed, because a distressed owner with positive equity always finds a buyer. A bust leg
(15% jobless, credit crunch) lifts that nine-fold and still lands an order of magnitude below
2014's 0.7%. If phase D's price formation — negative equity as a seller's reservation, search
over several listings — does not close that gap, this hold-out will fail on the foreclosure
leg, and that is the prediction being made in advance rather than after the fact.
